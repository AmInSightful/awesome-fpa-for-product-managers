# FP&A (Financial Planning & Analysis)

FP&A (Financial Planning & Analysis) is the discipline of planning, forecasting, analyzing, and communicating business performance to support better decisions.

For Product Managers, FP&A is the bridge between **product strategy and business outcomes**.

A strong PM does not only ask:

> "What should we build?"

They also ask:

> "How will this impact revenue, costs, customers, and company goals?"

---

# Why PMs Should Care About FP&A

Product decisions influence almost every major FP&A area:

| Product Decision | FP&A Impact |
|---|---|
| Launching a new product | Revenue forecast |
| Adding engineers | Headcount planning |
| Changing pricing | Revenue model |
| Improving retention | Customer lifetime value |
| Building infrastructure | Cost planning |
| Entering a market | Investment analysis |

---

# The FP&A Cycle

The typical FP&A process follows a continuous loop:

```
Strategy

↓

Planning

↓

Budgeting

↓

Forecasting

↓

Actual Results

↓

Variance Analysis

↓

Decision Making

↓

New Strategy
```

# Core FP&A Activities

## Annual Planning

Annual planning defines company goals and resource allocation for the next year.

Typical outputs:

- Revenue targets
- Expense budgets
- Hiring plans

- Strategic initiatives

# Forecasting

Forecasting estimates future business performance.

Common forecasts:

- Revenue forecast
- Customer growth forecast
- Cost forecast
- Headcount forecast
- Cash flow forecast

Modern companies increasingly use:

- Driver-based forecasting
- Scenario modeling
- AI-assisted forecasting

---

# Driver-Based Planning

Driver-based planning connects business outcomes to measurable inputs.

Example:

Instead of:

"Revenue will grow 30%"

A driver-based model asks:

```
Customers
    ×
Conversion Rate
    ×
Average Revenue Per User
    =
Revenue
```

For SaaS:

```
New Customers
+
Expansion Revenue
-
Churn
=
Ending ARR
```

---

# Rolling Forecasts

Traditional budgeting happens once per year.

Rolling forecasts continuously update predictions.

Example:

Every quarter:

- Review actual performance
- Update assumptions
- Adjust forecast
- Reallocate resources

This is common in:

- SaaS companies
- High-growth startups
- Technology companies

---

# Variance Analysis

Variance analysis compares:

```
Actual Results
        vs
Planned Results
```

Example:

Forecast:

```
ARR: $10M
```

Actual:

```
ARR: $8M
```

Questions:

- Why did we miss?
- Was the assumption wrong?
- Was execution weak?
- Did market conditions change?

---

# Headcount Planning

People are usually the largest cost in technology companies.

FP&A helps answer:

- How many engineers should we hire?
- When should we hire?
- What revenue impact is expected?
- Can we afford the investment?
