# Financial Statements

Understanding financial statements is one of the highest-leverage skills for Product Managers.

Financial statements explain **how a company creates value, spends money, and generates profit**. Every major product decision eventually appears somewhere in these statements.

---

# Why PMs Should Care

Product Managers influence:

- Revenue
- Costs
- Gross Margin
- Customer Acquisition
- Customer Retention
- Capital Allocation
- Operating Expenses

A PM who understands financial statements can better justify roadmap investments, pricing changes, and strategic initiatives.

---

# The Three Financial Statements

```
               Business Activity
                      │
                      ▼
          Income Statement (Profit)
                      │
                      ▼
           Balance Sheet (Assets)
                      │
                      ▼
         Cash Flow Statement (Cash)
```

Together, these statements tell the financial story of a business.

---

# Income Statement

The Income Statement (also called the Profit & Loss or P&L statement) shows whether a company made or lost money over a period of time.

Typical structure:

```
Revenue

− Cost of Revenue

= Gross Profit

− Operating Expenses

= Operating Income

− Interest

− Taxes

= Net Income
```

## Why PMs Should Care

Many product decisions directly affect:

- Revenue growth
- Gross Margin
- Customer Support costs
- Infrastructure costs
- Marketing efficiency

Example:

Adding a premium feature may increase Revenue but also increase infrastructure costs.

---

# Balance Sheet

The Balance Sheet is a snapshot of what the company owns and owes.

```
Assets = Liabilities + Equity
```

Assets include:

- Cash
- Inventory
- Accounts Receivable
- Equipment

Liabilities include:

- Debt
- Accounts Payable

Equity represents shareholder ownership.

## Why PMs Should Care

Product investments often require:

- Hiring engineers
- Purchasing infrastructure
- Investing in AI compute
- Long-term R&D

These investments influence the Balance Sheet.

---

# Cash Flow Statement

Profit does not equal cash.

The Cash Flow Statement explains where cash comes from and where it goes.

Three sections:

- Operating Activities
- Investing Activities
- Financing Activities

Many fast-growing SaaS companies are profitable on paper but consume significant cash.

---

# Gross Margin

One of the most important metrics for Product Managers.

```
Gross Margin = (Revenue − Cost of Revenue) / Revenue
```

High-margin products usually have:

- Strong pricing
- Efficient infrastructure
- Scalable operations

---

# Operating Expenses (OpEx)

Examples include:

- Engineering
- Product
- Sales
- Marketing
- Customer Success
- General & Administrative

PMs influence OpEx through prioritization and investment decisions.

---

# Capital Expenditures (CapEx)

CapEx represents long-term investments.

Examples:

- Data centers
- AI infrastructure
- Servers
- Office buildings

Cloud-native companies often reduce CapEx by renting infrastructure instead of owning it.

---

# Reading a SaaS Income Statement

Typical SaaS flow:

```
Revenue

↓

Cost of Revenue

↓

Gross Margin

↓

R&D

↓

Sales & Marketing

↓

General & Administrative

↓

Operating Income

↓

Net Income
```

Understanding this flow helps PMs explain how product initiatives impact company performance.

---

# Common Mistakes

❌ Profit = Cash

❌ Revenue = Profit

❌ Growth solves everything

❌ Cutting costs always improves business

❌ Gross Margin is only a finance metric

# Key Takeaways

If Product Managers learn only one finance topic, it should be financial statements.

Everything else—forecasting, pricing, budgeting, unit economics, and investment decisions—builds on understanding how business performance is reflected in the Income Statement, Balance Sheet, and Cash Flow Statement.
