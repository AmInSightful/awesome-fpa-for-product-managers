# Fundamentals

Before diving into financial modeling, forecasting, or pricing, every Product Manager should build a solid foundation in business and finance.

These concepts help answer questions like:

- Is this product creating value?
- Should we invest in this initiative?
- How do executives evaluate product performance?
- Why do finance and product teams prioritize differently?
- What makes a product financially successful?

---

# Learning Roadmap

If you're new to finance, follow this order:

1. Business Strategy
2. Financial Statements
3. Corporate Finance
4. Managerial Accounting
5. Value Creation
6. Opportunity Cost
7. Return on Investment (ROI)
8. Unit Economics
9. Strategic Finance
10. FP&A

# Core Concepts

## Business Strategy

Business strategy determines **where a company competes and how it wins**.

A great product without a great business strategy rarely becomes a great business.

### Why PMs should care

Product decisions should support company strategy—not just user requests.

Recommended topics:

- Competitive Advantage
- Business Models
- Market Positioning
- Network Effects
- Moats

---

## Corporate Finance

Corporate finance focuses on how companies invest money to maximize long-term value.

Topics include:

- Investments
- Capital Allocation
- Funding
- Cost of Capital
- Risk vs Return

### Why PMs should care

Every roadmap decision competes for limited company resources.

---

## Managerial Accounting

Unlike financial accounting, managerial accounting helps managers make internal decisions.

Topics include:

- Product profitability
- Cost allocation
- Budgets
- Variance analysis
- Decision support

---

## Strategic Finance

Strategic finance connects finance with company strategy.

Instead of asking:

> "What happened?"

Strategic finance asks:

> "What should we do next?"

---

## Value Creation

Products create value by generating benefits greater than their costs.

Common forms include:

- Revenue growth
- Cost reduction
- Risk reduction
- Time savings
- Customer retention

---

## Opportunity Cost

Every decision means saying no to another.

Choosing Feature A usually means delaying Feature B.

Opportunity cost is one of the most important concepts in product management.

---

## Return on Investment (ROI)

ROI measures the value created compared to the investment required.

Simple formula:

ROI = (Gain − Cost) / Cost

PMs frequently estimate ROI before prioritizing initiatives.

---

# Essential Skills for PMs

A financially strong Product Manager should be comfortable with:

- Reading financial statements
- Building simple business cases
- Estimating ROI
- Understanding SaaS metrics
- Forecasting revenue
- Pricing products
- Calculating unit economics
- Prioritizing investments
- Presenting financial trade-offs
